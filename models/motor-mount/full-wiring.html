Right motor and driver

ZS-X11H V2 connector orientation

View the component side with the board lettering upright and the white plugs along the bottom edge, as in your annotated photo.

Fit the external PWM jumper. Verify that zero PWM and an unplugged or resetting ESP32 leave the wheel stopped. Use the actual motor pinout for phase correspondence and Hall harness order. Inspect the connector from the board side shown here, since the wire-side view reverses pin order.

U1 and U2 use TI’s SN74LVC3G17 DCT or DCU eight-pin package numbering. Use one buffer per wheel and place its 100 nF capacitor close to pins 8 and 4. Measure the Hall levels while they are connected to the drivers and confirm the buffer input ratings. Q1 and Q2 are shown by emitter, base and collector. Match those names to the pinout of the package you have.

Pi, USB and optional altimeter

The Pi and ESP32 share one USB cable for wheel-speed commands, paired wheel-position moves and Hall feedback. See the proposed wheel control loops.

Use the BMP390’s four-pin JST-SH to female DuPont cable. Plug the female ends straight onto the Pi’s physical header pins shown above. The microphone, lidar adapter and ESP32 each use a Pi USB port. The powered speaker uses USB for power and the 3.5 mm jack for audio.
