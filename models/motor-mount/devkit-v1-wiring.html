Hall buffer and direction circuit

  • Keep the driver’s Hall 5 V and Hall GND with its motor harness. Branch only HA, HB and HC into the buffer. Place each 100 nF capacitor close to its buffer.
  • Verify the controller’s PWM input accepts a 3.3 V signal and has no pull-up above 3.3 V before connecting it directly. Verify the 10 kΩ pull-down holds the wheel still through ESP32 reset.
  • Move the right Rotation control wire to D23 with motor power off. Leave the old D2 connection unplugged because GPIO2 is sampled during boot. Use the shown ground-switch circuit and verify your transistor’s physical pin order.

Battery, return and USB

Verify the battery’s full-charge voltage and the power protection against the ratings of the installed controllers and wiring. The fuse and disconnect positions are shown schematically. Their ratings depend on your hardware.

Tune the wheel PID loops

Calibrate each Hall input

  1. Raise both wheels. Power the Hall sensors and leave the joystick released. Open the ESP32 serial terminal at 115200 baud.
  2. Type show. Mark one wheel, turn it by hand for five complete revolutions, then type show again. Only that wheel’s pulse count should increase.
  3. Divide the increase by five. Enter ppr l <count> for the left wheel or ppr r <count> for the right. For example, 75 counts over five turns gives ppr l 15 if it was the left wheel. Repeat for the other wheel, then enter save.

Driving becomes available after both counts are entered. If neither counter changes, check Hall power, the common ground, and the buffer output on D22 or D18 with the wheel turning.

Set a repeatable speed step

  1. Join HotelBot Wi-Fi and open 192.168.4.1. Set the speed limit to 30 RPM, then hold the joystick all the way forward for a straight, repeatable test. Release it before changing gains.
  2. Type log on to see target RPM, measured RPM and PWM for each wheel. The browser shows estimated and target RPM. The estimate uses Hall pulses for speed and the active direction output for the sign. Use log off when finished.
  3. Change one wheel at a time. To start with just proportional control, enter pid l 0.005 0 0 or pid r 0.005 0 0. The three numbers are Kp, Ki, Kd in that order.

Choose the gains

  1. Kp controls how quickly the wheel reacts. Raise it in small steps if the response is slow. Lower it if RPM repeatedly overshoots or hunts around the target.
  2. Ki removes steady speed error. Keep the Kp you chose and try a small Ki, such as pid l 0.005 0.005 0 if the left Kp is still 0.005. Increase Ki gradually if RPM settles below the target with PWM still below the cap. Reduce Ki if the wheel overshoots and takes longer to settle.
  3. Start with Kd at zero. It reacts to changes in measured RPM and can amplify Hall timing noise. Repeat the tuning on the other wheel. Recheck at low speed under load, then enter save.

If PWM stays at the 55% firmware cap and RPM is still low, lower the test target and check for load, supply or startup problems. If the UI reports a Hall fault, check the affected counter by hand and tap Clear Hall fault once feedback is restored.

Drive with Hall override allows limited drive after a Hall fault. The affected wheel uses open-loop PWM capped at 30% and its RPM is unknown. Use Stop to cancel the override before tuning.
